## Photo 1 — `67d1ea2d-...jpg` (handwritten, Victory notebook)

- Institutional approach in political science: how does the design of formal
  political institutions affect (a) strategies of political actors and
  (b) the probability distribution of outcomes. [core question, circled]
- Design differences in institutional arrangement make a difference in a
  nation's politics. Difference between inter- and intra-regime features.
  - a. Inter: authoritarian vs. democratic regime.
  - b. Intra (democratic regime): electoral system, forms of government,
    party system.
- Formal = written rules; most basic formal rules are in the constitution
  (especially in democracy). Constitution contains:
  - a. fundamental rules defining; b. the nature of the state;
  - c. the state vs. among other states;
  - d. the relations among different state organs;
  - e. the relations between state and its citizens.
- Below the constitution: legislation passed, policies enacted, the legal
  system regulating political/economic/social life; contracts by economic
  agents (incl. the state); by-laws of social orgs and firms.
- Political institutions = parchment institutions: making, implementing, and
  adjudicating policies binding for all members of a polity; in democracy
  includes access for contestation of top offices (rules and behavior).
  - of stateness (binding of all); of government (law-making,
    law-implementing, law-interpreting organs); of politics (struggle to make
    or influence binding policies).

## Photo 2 — `05153d43-...jpg` (handwritten)

- Whole ensemble of government: 3 branches → administrative, coercive, and
  regulatory apparatuses.
- PH presidential democracy parts: executive branch w/ departments and line
  agencies; Congress; Supreme Court and lower courts; constitutional bodies
  (COA, COMELEC, Ombudsman); other government entities.
- Representation of interests + contestation of government policies:
  - continuous organizational existence: party system → political parties are
    unique, the only organization acknowledged by the state to compete for
    the power to formulate policies binding to all.
  - periodic organizational expression: electoral system (COMELEC) → permanent
    constitutional body overseeing electoral rules; operationalization via
    elections is only periodic.
- Strategies of political actors via patterns of representation/contestation:
  - a. dictatorship: underground → extra-legal struggle, polarization of
    issues;
  - b. democracy: open political/electoral struggle; catch-all approach.
- In a democracy, the central institution structuring representation and
  contestation is the ELECTORAL SYSTEM. [circled, underlined]

## Photo 3 — `25c29c85-...jpg` (handwritten)

- Electoral system; the following are intimately related to the type of
  electoral system a democracy has: kind of executive-legislative relations;
  level of political accountability, transparency, governability; type of
  party system, internal party coherence and discipline; inclusiveness of
  the political system.
- Probability distribution of outcomes: different institutions produce
  different probability outcomes for different strategies.
  - a. Dictatorship: "predetermined outcomes" → electoral rules + dismal
    state of civil liberties → extremely difficult for the opposition to win
    the top post.
  - b. Democracy: "organized uncertainty" → electoral competition rules allow
    the opposition to win, incumbent to lose.

## Photo 4 — `4d58db99-...jpg` (printout, PPSJ p. 91, highlighted)

- Barebone definition: rules influencing strategies of state/social actors;
  two essential elements: (1) rules, (2) behaviors/strategies they structure.
  [annotated "rules/behaviors"]
- New institutional economics vs. new institutionalism (political science):
  economics counts formal + informal; political science = formal only
  (March & Olsen; "parchment institutions" — Carey; written as laws,
  regulations, constitutions, treaties).
- Constitution = most basic formal rules (nature of state, state vs. states,
  among organs, state-citizens); below: legislation, policies, legal system,
  contracts, by-laws. [annotated "memorize"]

## Photo 6 — `3b77d0ab-...jpg` (printout, PPSJ pp. 94–95, highlighted)

- Two answers: (1) institutions as incentives/constraints; (2) institutions
  as mediating mechanisms (state-society, state-economy, state-external).
- Different strategies: patterns of representation/contestation; time
  horizons (Przeworski 1991:19 — temporal cost-benefit; longer horizon →
  more willingness to play by rules).
- Regime distinction: authoritarian = extra-legal struggle + polarization;
  democracy = lowered costs of losing (no jail/death; another chance in
  3/4/6 years) → compliance. [annotated "authoritarian = extra legal
  struggle", "polarization of issue"]
- Parliamentary superiority claim: no lame-duck presidents (Cheibub).

## Photo 7 — `3baf18ed-...jpg` (printout, PPSJ p. 100, highlighted)

- Institutional blindness: all variants are society-based (state policies
  explained by configuration of social forces); institutions treated as
  epiphenomena — "passive structures shaped and acted upon by autonomous
  social actors"; studying them "not considered worthy of sustained focus."
- Diagram 1: State as Configuration of Social Forces (Marxist/class,
  Migdalian/society, Weberian/elites, Hybrid/all).

## Photo 8 — `45ddcb3c-...jpg` (printout, PPSJ p. 102, highlighted)

- Hutchcroft 1989:424 — dependent actor → "serious" analysis must begin with
  social interests acting upon the state.
- Hybrid approaches = collage (classes, elites, interest groups, civil
  society, globalization actors, masses); advance actors but NOT design
  analysis (Alegre, Clarke, Kerkvliet, Melegrito & Mendoza, Rocamora,
  Wui & Lopez).
- Institutional seeing: state as ensemble of institutions (incentives,
  constraints, mediation); design has a major role in social permeation.
  [key sentence highlighted]
